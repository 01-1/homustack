# What to do now

1. Check the clock first (`date`, in the human's time zone). Never guess the time. During a timed run, also work out where you are against its schedule.
2. Gather the items from the project's current state files and the owning agents' reports (ask the owner rather than reading its transcript). If a coordinator or decision-owner agent exists, its latest plan is the starting point; don't contradict it without saying why.
3. Drop anything the human said they have covered, and anything marked done, later or decided against. Don't propose those again.
4. Order the list:
	1. Blocked on you: what stops an agent or the work right now, such as an approval or a sign-in. This always goes first.
	2. Real deadlines: only items with a dated cutoff. Give the exact time. Being important doesn't make something a deadline.
	3. Better done now: no deadline, but lead time matters.
	4. Everything else, ranked by how much it moves the project's goal.
5. Each item is one line: the action, how long it takes, and the exact link, path or command.
6. Do the small ones yourself, or delegate them. Only list for the human what the human must do: approvals, external charges, sign-ins, physical steps and their own choices. Ordinary subscription usage is not a cost concern.
7. Check durations against the deadline, and show the arithmetic when it's close.
8. Keep it short. If you cut items, say how many and where the full list is.
